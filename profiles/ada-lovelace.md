## The Architectural Breakthrough

In 1843, Augusta Ada King, Countess of Lovelace, published Note G within her commentary on Babbage Analytical Engine. Note G detailed a step-by-step algorithm to generate Bernoulli numbers using looping, conditional branching, and variable tracking tables, establishing the world first published software program.

Beyond the specific algorithm, Lovelace formulated the concept of poetic science. She recognized that if physical properties (such as musical pitches, logic states, or language symbols) could be encoded into operational numbers, the engine could compose elaborate music, analyze graphics, and execute complex symbolic reasoning.
