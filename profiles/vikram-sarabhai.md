## The Architectural Breakthrough

In 1962, Dr. Vikram Sarabhai established the Indian National Committee for Space Research (INCOSPAR), which evolved into ISRO in 1969. While global superpowers pursued the Cold War Moon race for geopolitical prestige, Sarabhai explicitly defined a distinct mission: using space technology to solve real human problems in agriculture, weather forecasting, and mass rural education.

In 1975, his vision culminated in the Satellite Instructional Television Experiment (SITE), using NASA ATS-6 satellite to beam educational agricultural and health programming directly into 2,400 remote Indian villages using community television sets, bypassing decades of terrestrial cabling lag.

## Modern Systems Relevance

Sarabhai frugal systems engineering model proves that resource constraints spark architectural elegance. ISRO ability to launch Mars Orbiter Mission (Mangalyaan) and Chandrayaan missions at a fraction of Western budgets demonstrates the power of clean architectural simplification.

## Attribution & Historical Context

Sarabhai established the Physical Research Laboratory (PRL) in 1947, initiated the Thumba Equatorial Rocket Launching Station in 1963, and founded ISRO in 1969.
