## Core Systems Takeaway

> "When geopolitical or economic sanctions deny access to proprietary monolithic supercomputers, assemble massively parallel clusters from commodity components."

**World-Changing Impact:** Architected India first indigenous supercomputer using massively parallel commodity microprocessors when geopolitical sanctions blocked imports.

***

## The Architectural Breakthrough

In 1988, after the United States blocked the export of a Cray X-MP supercomputer to India for weather forecasting, the Indian government established C-DAC and appointed Dr. Vijay Bhatkar to lead the indigenous supercomputing program. With a fraction of Cray budget and a 3-year deadline, Bhatkar realized building a single giant proprietary processor was unviable.

Instead, Bhatkar pioneered massively parallel processing (MPP) using clusters of Inmos T800 transputers (commodity microprocessors). In 1991, C-DAC unveiled the PARAM 8000. Benchmarks demonstrated that the 64-node parallel cluster matched Cray performance at approximately 5% of the total capital cost.
