**World-Changing Impact:** Mathematized information, quantified data entropy, and proved error-free digital communication across noisy physical channels.

***

## The Architectural Breakthrough

In 1937 at MIT, Claude Shannon demonstrated that electric relay switches could implement every operation of Boolean algebra, laying the hardware foundation for all digital electronic circuit design. In 1948 at Bell Labs, he published A Mathematical Theory of Communication, defining the bit as the fundamental unit of information and formulating the Shannon-Hartley theorem.

Shannon proved that every physical channel possesses a maximum informational transmission capacity (C). As long as data transmission rate is below this limit, error-correcting codes can achieve arbitrarily reliable transmission, even across extremely noisy physical mediums.
