## The Architectural Breakthrough

In 1944, theoretical physicist Homi Jehangir Bhabha wrote to the Sir Dorabji Tata Trust proposing an institute for fundamental physics before India achieved independence. Recognizing that India possessed vast reserves of thorium (monazite sands) but scarce domestic uranium, Bhabha conceived the three-stage nuclear power programme.

The architecture was brilliant: Stage 1 uses natural uranium in Heavy Water Reactors to produce plutonium; Stage 2 uses Fast Breeder Reactors with plutonium to breed U-233 from thorium; Stage 3 uses U-233 with thorium to produce self-sustaining, long-term energy independence.

## Attribution & Historical Context

Bhabha formulated relativistic electron-positron scattering (Bhabha scattering) in 1935 and founded the Tata Institute of Fundamental Research (TIFR) and Atomic Energy Establishment, Trombay (now BARC).
