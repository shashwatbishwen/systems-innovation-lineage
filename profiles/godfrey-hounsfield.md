## Core Systems Takeaway

> "When a direct physical view into an internal system is impossible, take hundreds of multi-angle boundary measurements and reconstruct the internal reality mathematically."

**World-Changing Impact:** Invented medical CT scanning, allowing clinicians to view three-dimensional soft-tissue cross-sections of the human body for the first time.

---

## The Architectural Breakthrough

Prior to 1971, X-rays were flat two-dimensional projections where dense bones obscured soft tissues like brain tumors and blood clots. Godfrey Hounsfield at EMI Laboratories conceived a radical reconstruction technique: rotating an X-ray source and detector around a patient head in small angular increments.

Hounsfield split the cross-sectional slice into a mathematical grid of pixels. By processing thousands of simultaneous linear equations on a minicomputer, his algorithm calculated the precise X-ray absorption coefficient for every internal voxel (measured today in Hounsfield Units), rendering internal soft tissue visible without exploratory surgery.

## Attribution & Historical Context

Hounsfield built the prototype CT scanner at EMI Central Research Laboratories; Allan Cormack independently developed the underlying Radon mathematical reconstruction equations.
