## The Architectural Breakthrough

In 1953 at Imperial College London, Narinder Singh Kapany solved a classic optical physics barrier: transmitting high-resolution light and image streams through curved, flexible glass strands without light leaking out at the cladding interface. Using high-index glass cores clad with lower-index materials, he achieved total internal reflection.

Kapany published his findings in Nature in 1954 and coined the term fibre optics in Scientific American in 1960. His research proved that optical fibers could carry orders of magnitude more bandwidth than copper wiring while remaining completely immune to electromagnetic interference.

---

## Modern Systems Relevance

Kapany optical waveguides form the physical backbones of modern transoceanic subsea cables and datacenter fiber interconnects that make modern multi-region cloud computing, cross-datacenter database replication, and global edge CDNs physically viable.

---

## Attribution & Historical Context

Kapany demonstrated high-transmission flexible optical fibers in 1953 at Imperial College London with Harold Hopkins, publishing the foundational 1954 Nature paper.
