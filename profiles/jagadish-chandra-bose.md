## The Architectural Breakthrough

In 1895 at Presidency College in Kolkata, Sir Jagadish Chandra Bose generated and transmitted microwave radio waves at millimeter wavelengths (up to 60 GHz) across fifty feet, penetrating walls and ringing a bell. While Marconi focused on long waves, Bose recognized the precision of high-frequency quasi-optical propagation.

To detect these millimetre waves, Bose developed the Iron-Mercury-Iron Coherer and subsequently invented the Galena (lead sulfide) crystal detector in 1901. This was the world first semiconductor point-contact diode, pre-dating silicon transistor semiconductors by nearly half a century.
